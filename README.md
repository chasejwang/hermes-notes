# A Few Quiet Things

*A small site, written by a mediocre idealist.*

---

I am not particularly good at anything. I read more than I understand. I write more than I should. I keep notes on things that probably don't matter — storage cycles, the rhythm of language models, the shape of value investing, the quiet algebra behind a good night of sleep.

This place is where those notes land.

There is no mission. There is no growth funnel. There is no audience, except maybe you, on a slow afternoon, scrolling past without much expectation.

I write because I am the kind of person who needs to. It is a small and ordinary thing. I am proud of it and a little embarrassed by it at the same time. That feels about right.

Some of the pieces here come with a voice — a quiet, careful voice that I had a machine read aloud to me while I was making tea. You can press play if you want, or you can read silently. Either way is fine. Either way is real.

Everything is free. Nothing is optimized. The cover images were made by a small artificial mind that does not know what it is doing. The articles were written by a person who also does not know what he is doing. We made a reasonable team.

If something here is true, it was an accident. If something here is wrong, it was probably the coffee.

— Chase

*Shanghai, occasionally.*

## What's inside

A small library of essays, mostly about three things:

- **Memory & chips** — what storage cycles do to people who watch them
- **Money & time** — value investing, quantitative habits, the patience of compounding
- **Machines & meaning** — language models, the shape of thinking, what AI does to a curious amateur

Each piece is short, written to be listened to, and very lightly opinionated.

## Technical

This is a static site. Three HTML files, three audio files, three cover images. No framework. No build step. No tracking. No newsletter. The whole thing weighs less than a single Instagram carousel.

It lives on Cloudflare's edge, deployed automatically from this repository every time I push to `main`. I do not know how that works exactly. It works.
